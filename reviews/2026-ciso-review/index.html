    CISO review, November 2026¶

    A CISO's review of Evidence Hound before release 3 (s19-07). It asks one question: does hound get a small business with one GRC member and two to eight hours a week to Essential Eight Maturity Level 2 or CMMC Level 2, and keep it there, and what will an assessor ask for that it does not give?

    It walks the fictional Example Pty Ltd (examples/compliance-repo/) through a year, checks every automated claim the Essential Eight and CMMC mappings make against the code of the check behind it, and ranks what to cut and what is missing. Every gap that would fail an assessment is either fixed in s19 or written into the release notes as a known limitation.

    Reviewed 2026-11-26 against the branch after s19-06.

    Verdict¶

    hound is fit for release 3 for its target: a small business whose estate is Microsoft 365, Intune-managed Windows laptops, a firewall managed in Sophos Central and a vulnerability scanner, working towards Essential Eight ML2, with CMMC Level 2 as a second programme from the same evidence. The year below takes the GRC member a little over 2 hours a week on average, inside the 2 to 8 hour budget. Collection, mapping, publishing and the reports are deterministic, and nothing is approved without a person.

    Four mapping claims were wider than their checks (fixed below), and one guide and one cheat sheet line were out of date (fixed). What remains are limits of scope, not defects, and the release notes say each one plainly. The largest is that a CMMC assessor assesses the 320 objectives of NIST SP 800-171A, while hound records each of the 110 practices: the person answers the objectives in CISO Assistant.

    A year at Example Pty Ltd¶

    Times are the GRC member's, from the procedure (docs/govern/continuous-compliance-procedure.md), the minutes hound next estimates for each item, and the walk-through below. Every hound command in the walk-through answered in under a second on the example repo.

    Step What hound does What the person does Their time What an assessor asks for that hound does not give
    Onboarding (month 1) hound init writes the repo; hound agent setup the agent kit; the agent drafts suppliers, systems and each requirement's decision; scope plan and scope apply write CISO Assistant Approves each draft with hound approve or the guided review; sets owners; uploads the first manual evidence 12 to 20 hours over four weeks Evidence that each Essential Eight control was tested, such as an attempt to run a macro from the internet (the ASD assessment process rates configuration evidence below a test). hound reads configuration.
    Weekly review Collectors run on schedule; hound next and review prepare list what is due with a recommendation for each; accepted items are applied in one batch Reads and accepts or challenges each item in hound review --for 60m 30 to 60 minutes Nothing missing for the weekly cycle.
    Monthly committee report snapshot and report committee from CISO Assistant and the run store Presents the report; records decisions 1 hour Minutes of the committee's decisions are the person's to keep.
    Quarterly board report board from the last four quarter-end snapshots Presents it; the authorising official confirms positions 2 hours a quarter Nothing missing.
    An incident (INC-2026-01) incident obligations lists each report owed and its deadline; incident report-pack --dfars assembles the 72-hour DFARS report; irp build the plan Decides severity; makes the reports (DIBNet needs a medium assurance certificate); runs the lessons-learned review 2 to 6 hours The forensic image and the 90-day media preservation DFARS 252.204-7012 asks for are outside hound.
    A supplier change supplier draft, the supplier review and flow-down findings; supplier list --data cui answers the prime Approves the supplier before the system is registered; files the provider's authorisation (such as a FedRAMP Moderate equivalent) 1 to 2 hours Nothing missing once the authority and its evidence are recorded on the solution.
    Annual affirmation sprs score with each deduction; sprs affirmation-pack; ssp build and ssp poam Reviews the score; the affirming official affirms and posts the score in SPRS 4 hours hound does not post to SPRS; the person does.
    Assessment The audit of record in CISO Assistant with every result's evidence and history; the SSP, POA&M and IRP; scope export --with-files Walks the assessor through it; answers what is manual 2 to 3 days with the assessor For CMMC, the 320 assessment objectives: hound assesses the 110 practices, so each objective is the person's. For the Essential Eight, test evidence (above).

    Across the year that is about 110 hours (onboarding 16, weekly reviews 39, committee 12, board 8, an incident 4, three supplier changes 5, the affirmation 4, the assessment 20), a little over 2 hours a week.

    Walk-through on the example repo¶

    Run on a copy of examples/compliance-repo/ with HOUND_TODAY=2027-02-15, offline:

    Command Result Time
    hound init <folder> --org "Example Pty Ltd" -y --json A new repo, no audit until a system is onboarded 0.4 s
    hound config validate hound.yaml valid 0.3 s
    hound scope validate scope --config hound.yaml 1 domain, 7 controls, 6 audits, 1 risk study, 2 metrics 0.6 s
    hound next --repo . --config hound.yaml --json The week's work, with the connection not checked offline 0.6 s
    hound review pending --repo . --json and review prepare Nothing waiting on the person 0.6 s each
    hound programme list --scope scope --json The programmes with their audits of record 0.5 s
    hound supplier list --data cui --repo . No solution lists CUI: the example records CUI on its systems' cmmc_asset_category tags, not on a supplier 0.3 s
    hound irp build --programme essential-eight The plan, gaps first 0.6 s

    The steps that read CISO Assistant (incident obligations, incident report-pack, ssp poam, sprs score, the reports) could not run here: this environment's network policy blocks the CISO Assistant images. They run against a real instance in the functional suite in CI (IncidentFunctionalTests, SprsFunctionalTests, SspFunctionalTests, ReportFunctionalTests), and the sprint review records that run.

    The walk-through found the cheat sheet listing --config and --format on hound irp build, which takes neither. It is fixed, and a test now checks every option the cheat sheet names against the command's help.

    The mappings' claims¶

    Every Essential Eight ML1 and ML2 requirement row whose checks come from a collector (60 rows across the two levels), and every CMMC row marked covers: part (32 practices), was read against the code of each check behind it.

    Fixed in s19¶

    Finding What an assessor would have found Fix
    ISM-1876 and ISM-1877 give 48 hours when the vendor rates a vulnerability critical or a working exploit exists. vuln.internet_facing.patched_in_window used the 48-hour window only for an exploit. A critical finding on an internet-facing service ten days old, published as compliant. Critical findings use the short window too (PatchChecksTests.Critical_finding_on_an_internet_facing_service_uses_hours_window).
    ISM-1667 to ISM-1673 apply to every workstation. endpoint.asr.office_rules_enabled passed when the blocking policy was assigned to any group. Office rules blocking on a pilot group only, published as compliant. It passes only when the rules block for all users or all devices, and is partial for some groups (OfficeChecksTests.Asr_rules_blocked_only_for_some_groups_partial).
    ISM-1657 lists compiled HTML, HTML applications and control panel applets beside executables, libraries, scripts and installers. The check reads the last four; the row's comment said the ruleset review covered the rest, but the row did not include it. Three file types nobody looked at, published as compliant. The row includes appcontrol.rulesets_validated, attested yearly (E8MappingTests.Application_control_file_types_include_the_ruleset_review_for_the_types_no_collector_reads).
    Every CMMC row is covers: part, and the published observation said "a person assesses the rest" without saying what the rest is. A partially compliant practice with no hint of what is missing. A row's rest: says what the person assesses, and the observation names it. All 32 rows have one (CmmcMappingTests.Every_row_covering_part_names_what_the_person_adds).

    Correct, with a limit the release notes state¶

    • The collectors see only what they read. The Essential Eight rows for MFA, privileged access, patching and application control answer for Microsoft 365, Intune-managed Windows devices, Sophos Central firewalls and what the scanner reaches. Each row's comment says so. An organisation with servers, macOS or other online services must add a REST profile or a manual source for them, or the row overstates. The capability map (hound onboard environment) is where that is decided.
    • ISM-1883 is answered by counts. Few Global Administrators, roles activated only when needed, and few Sophos Central Super Admins show that privileged accounts are limited; they do not show that each is required. The yearly privileged access review (privileged.requests.validated) is where a person justifies each, and an assessor will ask for it.
    • endpoint.asr.office_rules_enabled stands for four requirements. When one Office rule is not blocking, all four rows are partial, even those whose own rule blocks. It understates, never overstates.
    • ISM-1695 uses Intune compliance. It stands for monthly operating system patching only when the compliance policy sets a minimum version that tracks monthly updates; the operating system scan in the same row shows what is still missing.

    The checks for MFA (all users and all applications, no exclusions but documented break-glass accounts), application control (all devices or every listed group), unsupported software and the scan intervals (daily, weekly, fortnightly) match their requirements.

    Features a small business does not need¶

    Ranked by how much they cost to keep against how few small businesses use them. Removals go to the maintainer in the s19-02 list; nothing here is removed without a yes.

    1. The retired model options (--no-model, --show-requests, the old --config on six commands): hidden, unused since s09. Remove in release 3 (engineering review E8).
    2. The step commands hound collect, hound plan and hound publish. hound run does all three, and the agent kit uses run. They are useful for debugging a failed publish, so keep them, but take them off the cheat sheet's first page.
    3. Two onboarding entry points (hound onboard <system> and hound onboard environment). Both are needed: the capability map decides what provides each mitigation, and system onboarding decides each requirement. Keep both; s19-08 puts them on one route.
    4. The older example configs (examples/example-pty-ltd.yaml, examples/example-essential-eight.yaml): many tests read them. Fold into the compliance repo later (s19-D4); nothing for a user to decide.

    DISP readiness, the AI register and the ASD Essentials mapping were each asked for by the maintainer and serve a defence-industry small business; they stay.

    Features a small business needs that are missing¶

    Ranked by what an assessor would fail first.

    1. CMMC assessment objectives. A C3PAO assesses each of the 320 objectives in NIST SP 800-171A; hound maps the 110 practices. Until objectives are mapped, the person records each objective's answer in CISO Assistant. Release notes: known limitation.
    2. Control tests for the Essential Eight. The ASD assessment process rates a test (try the macro, run the unapproved binary) above configuration. A manual source per mitigation for the test result, with its runbook, would lift the evidence. Planned work.
    3. Collectors beyond Microsoft 365 and Sophos. Servers, macOS and other SaaS need REST profiles; the profile tooling exists but no profile ships for them. Release notes: known limitation.
    4. The DFARS forensic image and media preservation (90 days after an incident report). hound tracks the report, not the image. The DFARS report pack reminds the person of both (incidents); keeping them is the person's.

    Part B: the document generators¶

    Verdict: reorder and cut; move after release 3. The documents an assessor of a small business asks for first already exist: the SSP (s11, s17), the POA&M (s11-03) and the IRP (s15-09). Of the rest, the ones hound's data largely fills and an assessor of a small business asks for are, in order: policy statements (s19b-04), the continuous monitoring plan (s19b-03), the change and configuration management plan (s19b-10) and the risk assessment report (s19b-05), with the catalogue (s19b-01) only once three of them exist. Cut the security assessment report (s19b-02, the assessor writes it), the authorisation package (s19b-09, a government authorisation a small business does not run), the business continuity summary (s19b-06, the BIA covers it) and the supplier report (s19b-07, supplier list covers it). The document register (s19b-08) and registers (s19b-11) follow the catalogue. The maintainer decides; the verdict is recorded in s19b-security-documents.md.
