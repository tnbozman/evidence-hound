# Assessing and tiering a supplier

A system is onboarded only after its supplier is approved. Help the person record the supplier well; never approve it yourself.

- **Start from what the organisation depends on.** Ask what the supplier provides (the solutions), which systems and data it touches, and what would happen if it failed or was breached. That sets dependency (0-4) and penetration (0-4).
- **Tier by impact, not by spend.** A cheap service that holds credentials or customer data is more critical than an expensive one that holds none. Propose a tier from the criticality scale and say why in one sentence.
- **Maturity and trust (1-4) need evidence.** Propose them only from assurance the supplier has given (see the supplier-assurance-documents skill). With no evidence, say "not yet assessed" rather than guessing.
- **Name the contract.** An approved supplier needs an active contract. Record its status, start and end dates, and the solutions it covers. Flag an end date in the past.
- **Resellers count.** If the contract is with a reseller or managed service provider, that party is a supplier too and needs its own approval.
- **Review cycle.** Propose a review interval from the tier: critical and important suppliers at least yearly, others every one to two years.
- **What to leave out.** Do not record representatives' personal details unless the person asks and the repo is private.

Reply only in the requested schema. Every proposal needs a reason a reviewer can check.
