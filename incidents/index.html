    Incidents and reporting deadlines¶

    This guide is for a GRC team that must report an incident to regulators and customers on their clocks: 72 hours to the DoD under DFARS 252.204-7012 when covered defence information is involved, 30 days to assess a data breach under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme, and the reports to ASD's ACSC. You declare those obligations once, in the compliance repo; the incident itself is recorded in CISO Assistant; and hound incident obligations tells you which reports one incident owes and by when.

    The example compliance repo has the obligations in examples/compliance-repo/scope/reporting.yaml and the plan's yearly test as a manual source (example-incident-response-plan in its hound.yaml, with the runbook examples/compliance-repo/manual-sources/incident-response-test.md).

    1. Declare what you must report¶

    scope/reporting.yaml lists each obligation: an id, who receives it (report_to), how long after the incident's discovery it is due (within, such as 72h or 30d), and what triggers it: when: {data: [cui]} for incidents on a system that handles those data kinds (the data of each system in scope/setup.yaml, see Scope files), or when: {always: true} for every incident.

    obligations:
  - id: dfars-7012
    name: DFARS 252.204-7012 cyber incident report
    report_to: DoD, through the DIBNet portal (needs a medium assurance certificate)
    within: 72h
    when: {data: [cui]}
    reference: DFARS 252.204-7012(c)
  - id: reportcyber
    name: Report to ASD's ACSC through ReportCyber
    report_to: ASD's ACSC (cyber.gov.au/report)
    within: 1d
    when: {always: true}

    Check each clock against its source, and add your customers' contract clauses the same way. hound scope validate checks the file.

    2. Record the incident in CISO Assistant¶

    Under Operations, Incidents, record the incident with a reference ID (such as INC-2026-01) and the affected systems as its assets. Add a timeline entry of type detection at the time it was discovered: every clock starts there (without one, when the incident was reported).

    3. List the reports it owes¶

    hound incident obligations INC-2026-01 --config hound.yaml

    INC-2026-01 Malware on a laptop: discovered 2026-11-20 09:30 UTC, 2 report(s) owed
  reportcyber  due 2026-11-21 09:30 UTC  made 2026-11-20 15:00 UTC  to ASD's ACSC (every incident)
  dfars-7012  due 2026-11-23 09:30 UTC  due  to DoD, through the DIBNet portal (Example laptops handles cui)

    --format json gives the same for an agent. The command reads CISO Assistant and writes nothing.

    The DFARS 252.204-7012 report¶

    When the incident touches a system that handles CUI, the DFARS report is due 72 hours from discovery and is filed by a person on the DoD portal (DIBNet), with a medium assurance certificate. To gather what it asks for:

    hound incident report-pack INC-2026-01 --dfars --config hound.yaml --out inc-2026-01-dfars.md

    The pack lists the deadline, the affected systems that handle CUI with their data and CMMC asset category, the suppliers whose solutions provide them and their contracts, the customer contracts that carry the clause (the primes to tell, from scope/third-parties/), and the incident's timeline. It reminds you to keep images of the affected systems and the monitoring data for at least 90 days, and to send any malicious software you isolate to the DoD Cyber Crime Center as the portal directs. The portal asks for more than the pack holds, such as your CAGE code. hound reads only; keep the pack out of the repo.

    4. Record each report you make¶

    Add a timeline entry to the incident that starts with Reported: and the obligation's id, then their reference: Reported: dfars-7012, DIBNet report 12345. The report then shows as made, and is never overdue.

    Where overdue reports show up¶

    • The weekly review. With CISO Assistant configured (HOUND_CA_URL and HOUND_CA_TOKEN), hound review lists each report an open incident has not made, due by its deadline, every week until the incident's timeline records it.
    • The committee report. hound report snapshot records the reports each incident owes; hound report committee lists the overdue ones in section 6 and proposes making each in section 8.

    Build the plan¶

    hound irp build --programme essential-eight writes the incident response plan from what the repo already holds, so you write only the parts that need judgement. scope/irp.yaml names the programme, the plan's version, who holds each role and the Markdown file of each judgement section:

    programme: essential-eight
version: "0.1"
roles:
  incident_manager: it@example.com    # leads a response
  deputy: IT team                     # stands in
  executive: ceo@example.com          # decides for the business, the only spokesperson
  legal_privacy: ceo@example.com      # law, contracts and privacy
  authorising_officer: ceo@example.com  # approves each version (ISM-0047)
sections:
  severity: irp/severity.md
  escalation: irp/escalation.md
  containment: irp/containment.md
  communication: irp/communication.md
  lessons_learned: irp/lessons-learned.md
    • Derived: the contacts from the roles, the systems with the sensitive data they handle and their recovery objectives (setup.yaml), the suppliers and customers with their contacts and contract clauses (third-parties/, including flowed-down clauses), and each report with its deadline and what triggers it (reporting.yaml). --json gives every value with its file and line.
    • Written by you: severity definitions, escalation decisions, containment priorities, communication approach and lessons learned. A missing role or section, or an empty file, is a gap listed first; --strict exits non-zero while any remain.
    • Layout: NIST SP 800-61 Rev 3's functions (govern, identify, protect, detect, respond, recover), with the Australian reporting (ReportCyber, the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme, DISP) and DFARS 252.204-7012 inside them, and a section for each of ISM-0043's eight items. It cites NIST SP 800-171 3.6.1 to 3.6.3 and the ACSC Cyber Incident Response Plan Guidance.

    The plan holds names and contacts: keep the built plan out of a public repo, and store the approved copy as the evidence of the yearly check below. Raise version when you change the plan; the authorising officer approves each version.

    The plan and its yearly test¶

    The incident response plan is approved and tested once a year, for example with a one-hour tabletop exercise. Make it a manual source with the checks incident.response_plan_approved and incident.response_plan_tested, due every 366 days, with a runbook and an evidence plan row (see Manual evidence), as the example repo does.

    What stays in CISO Assistant¶

    The incidents, their timelines, severity and status are recorded in CISO Assistant, never in the repo. The repo holds only the obligations: who must be told, and how soon.
