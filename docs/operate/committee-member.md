# Reading the committee report

Each month you get one report, about five pages, the week before the security committee meets. Read it in 15 minutes this way.

1. **Summary first.** Three to five lines: what changed and what you must decide.
2. **Section 8, Actions.** These are proposed: an action, an owner and a due date. Be ready to agree, change or drop each one.
3. **Section 4, Exceptions and risk acceptances.** Anything expiring within 60 days needs a renewal decision, or a plan to fix the
   underlying problem.
4. **Section 7, Overdue evidence.** Owners overdue for a second month are listed first; the committee is where that is followed up.
5. The rest when you have questions. Every figure links to its record in CISO Assistant, if you have a login.

## Words you will see

| Word | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Programme | One framework you report against, such as the Essential Eight at Maturity Level Two |
| Audit of record | The CISO Assistant audit whose results count for that programme |
| Compliant, partially, non compliant, not assessed | The result of a requirement in that audit; not assessed means the evidence is missing or old |
| Collector | A program that reads a system (Microsoft 365, the firewall) and records evidence on a schedule |
| Stale | A collector with no run in the last 7 days of the month; its evidence may be out of date |
| Manual evidence | Evidence a person exports because no API shows it, attested by the GRC team |
| Exception | Approval not to meet a requirement for a time, with a reason and an expiry |
| Risk acceptance | Approval to live with a risk for a time, by someone with the authority to accept it |

## In the meeting

Decide, and make sure each decision has an owner and a date. The GRC member records it in CISO Assistant; next month's report shows
whether it happened. Training: [committee session](../training/committee.md).
