# System owner

You own a system (a file share, the laptops, the accounting service) and some of its evidence has no API, so a person has to export it.
You do not need a CISO Assistant login. You need about ten minutes each time a reminder arrives.

## When the reminder arrives

CISO Assistant emails you before each due date, with a runbook: what to export, from where, and what the export must show.

1. Follow the runbook. Export exactly what it asks for (a report, a screenshot of a setting, a list of accounts), dated today.
2. Check it shows what the runbook says it must, for example every server in the backup report, not only the first page.
3. Send it to the GRC team as the runbook says (usually by email or a shared folder). Do not paste it into chat tools.
4. If you cannot do it by the due date, reply to the email with the reason and a date. Do not ignore it: at month end an open task is
   reported to the security committee with your name.

The GRC team's agent reads the export and proposes a result; a person in the GRC team attests it. You may be asked a question about it.

## Find your audit and your actions

If you have a CISO Assistant login (some owners do):

- **Your audit:** Compliance, then Audits, then the audit of record (for example `EX-E8-2026`). Filter the requirements by your
  system's applied controls to see the rows your evidence answers.
- **Your actions:** Applied controls with you as owner, sorted by ETA. Each one the committee assigned you has a due date.
- **Update an action:** change its status and ETA, and add a note on what changed. Do not change results in the audit: the runs write
  those, and a hand edit is overwritten or reported as drift.

No login: ask the GRC member for the list of your actions; they are in each month's committee report under section 8.

## When something changes

Tell the GRC team before you: add a new system or supplier, connect a new AI feature, move data to a new place, or change who can sign in
as an administrator. Each of these changes your audit's scope, and some must be assessed before they go live.
